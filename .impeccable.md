## Design Context

### Users
Broad productivity audience — students, remote workers, developers, professionals. Anyone building better focus habits through structured work sessions. Used across desktop, mobile, and browser during work/study hours. Users range from casual to power users tracking detailed analytics.

### Brand Personality
**Calm, trustworthy, steady.** Like a reliable coach who speaks clearly and doesn't oversell. Professional without being corporate. Confident without being flashy. The interface should feel like a tool you trust with your time.

Three words: **composed, dependable, clear.**

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Theme**: Dark (current) — suits focused work contexts and extended screen time
- **Tone**: Measured confidence. Not minimal to the point of emptiness, not dense to the point of overwhelm. Information-rich but visually quiet.
- **Anti-references**: Avoid gamified/achievement-heavy aesthetics (no confetti, streaks, badges). Avoid neon-on-dark "developer tool" cliches. Avoid wellness/soft pastel territory.
- **Open to creative direction** — no rigid visual references to match

### Design Principles
1. **Clarity over decoration** — Every element should communicate, not decorate. If it doesn't help the user understand or act, remove it.
2. **Calm confidence** — The interface should feel steady and assured. No urgency tricks, no anxiety-inducing patterns. Trust the user.
3. **Information density done right** — Show meaningful data without visual noise. Good hierarchy lets density feel spacious.
4. **Consistency across surfaces** — Desktop, mobile, browser extension, and web should feel like one product with one voice.
5. **Earn attention, don't demand it** — Subtle motion, purposeful color, quiet transitions. The interface recedes; the user's focus stays on their work.
